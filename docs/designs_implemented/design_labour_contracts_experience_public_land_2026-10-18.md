# Labour contracts, experience and public land — as implemented (17–18 October 2026)

*Design notes written after the fact, from Stef's specification and the code. Switches and parameters in `src/parameters.jl`.*

## Contracts (`contracts`, on the ladder's base)
Contracts go both ways and take hours off the market. A temp who has worked `contract_tenure_months` (3) running for an employer gets a contract if the employer still has room under `contract_share` (the share of its hands it may hold on contract; the rest are temps, always on the market). Each month the employer takes the contracted units first, at the contract wage, before the market opens — the contract is a floor, not a cap: the worker's other hours go to the market as a temp's, and the same employer can hire them for more. The contract wage follows the price of bread by `contract_indexation` (1 = fully). An employer that wants fewer hands serves `notice_months` (3) and keeps paying; a worker who could earn `worker_leave_margin` (10 %) more at the going wage gives `worker_notice_months` (1) and leaves, though not while bread or hands are short. A contract ends with its employer. The first version pinned contracted workers to fixed units at a fixed wage and took them off the market altogether; it starved the SuMSy village (22 alive with everyone on contract), because the emergency could not reach them — hence the floor and the indexation.

## Experience (`experience`, a switch)
Months worked running for one kind of enterprise build experience: level 2 after `experience_months[1]` (3), level 3 after `experience_months[2]` (9); every `experience_decay_months` (3) not worked in that kind cost a level. A unit of an experienced worker's labour is worth `1 + experience_bonus[level − 1]` (+10 %, +20 %) to an employer of that kind — in the harvest, the baking and the seats (`effective_labour`) — and employers order the pool by asking wage divided by that factor, so an experienced worker is hired first at equal cost (loyalty keeps its place among equals). The first version read a list that holds only the government's hires, so every firm's effective labour was zero and the village died by month 4; it now reads the hires themselves.

## The joint labour market (`joint_labour_market`, on the ladder)
Farms and banks hire before the harvest; then, after the grain market, bakeries and theatres hire in one market, in turns, a unit each per pass, so neither can take the hands the other needs. A first version put all four kinds in one market before the harvest; bakeries paid for hands with no grain to bake, month 1 baked nothing, and the debt village fell to 434 alive.

## Public land (`public_land`, an option exclusive with land ownership)
The government owns the land; nobody else does, so there is no land market, no land in wealth and no levy (`land_levy` or `land_pricing = :market` with it is refused). Farms are allotted the plots they work, round robin up to the stock. Three ground rents in loaves, all zero by default and scaled with the tax levers, are paid to the government as tax: per plot a farm works, per bakery or theatre, per villager for the ground they live on. Two policies for those on the benefit: `public_land_dwelling_exempt_benefit` (they pay no dwelling rent) and `public_land_dwelling_in_benefit` (the rent is added to the benefit). Gesell's lease to the highest bidder is not modelled. In the first ten runs a quarter-loaf dwelling rent on a benefit of exactly two loaves starved the debt village (239 alive); without the rents public land did the debt village good (494 alive, the gap 38 meals instead of 538).
